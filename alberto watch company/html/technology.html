The Heart Of Every Watch

The movement is the engine inside a watch. Choose a type to see how it works.

Mechanical (hand-wound)

A coiled spring called the mainspring stores energy. You wind it by hand every day or two, and tiny gears release that energy slowly to move the hands. Many collectors love it for the craftsmanship and the daily winding ritual.

INSIDE THE CASE

How A Mechanical Watch Works

Five parts work together in order. Click a step, or watch it move on its own.

Mainspring

A tightly coiled metal spring stores the energy. Winding the crown, or the rotor in an automatic watch, tightens it.

Gear train

A chain of small gears carries the energy from the spring and slows it down, so the hands move at the right speed.

Escapement

This part lets the energy out in tiny, equal steps. It makes the ticking sound you hear in a mechanical watch.

Balance wheel

A small wheel swings back and forth, usually about four times a second. It sets the rhythm that controls the accuracy of the watch.

Hands and dial

The last gears turn the hour, minute and second hands, so you can read the time on the dial.

MATERIALS

Built From Quality Parts

Sapphire crystal

The glass over the dial is extremely hard, rating 9 on the Mohs scale, so everyday scratches are rare.

316L stainless steel

A strong steel that resists rust and corrosion, used for cases and bracelets.

Ceramic bezel

The ring around the dial keeps its color in sunlight and resists scratches.

Titanium

Much lighter than steel and gentle on the skin, ideal for large watches worn all day.

Luminous markers

A special coating on the hands and hour marks glows in the dark so you can read the time at night.

Leather and rubber straps

Choose soft leather for dress watches or rubber for sport and swimming.
